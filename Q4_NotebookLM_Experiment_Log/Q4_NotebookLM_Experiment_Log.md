# Q4 NotebookLM Experiment Log

## Experiment Title

Evaluating NotebookLM for ESG Reporting Standards Study Support

## Assessment Context

This experiment supports Question 4 of the assessment. The task was to evaluate Google NotebookLM or a similar AI-powered note-taking assistant for use in a university learning ecosystem. The evaluation used an ESG-related academic scenario.

## Tool Used

Google NotebookLM free evaluation access.

## Date of Experiment

June 2026

## Notebook Title

ESG Reporting Standards Briefing

## Aim of Experiment

The aim was to evaluate whether NotebookLM can support students, lecturers and researchers with source-based summarisation, question answering, comparison, revision support and theme extraction.

---

## Academic Scenario

The scenario was preparing an ESG reporting standards briefing for university students. The notebook was used to summarise ESG reporting standards, compare reporting frameworks and generate study support materials.

2. IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards

NotebookLM accepted the sources and generated an initial source-grounded notebook overview.

- TCFD focuses on climate-related financial disclosures.

### Observation

This was one of the strongest outputs because it helped distinguish the purpose and audience of the three frameworks.

## Accuracy and Relevance

NotebookLM was mostly accurate when the answer was clearly supported by the uploaded ESG sources. Its outputs were relevant to the academic scenario because they helped summarise, compare and organise ESG reporting information.

However, some outputs were broad and needed follow-up questions. For example, the first overview explained ESG reporting generally but did not fully explain the differences between GRI, IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards and TCFD until a comparison prompt was used.

---

## Evidence Quality Evaluation

NotebookLM’s citation markers improved evidence quality because the user could check where information came from. This made the output more transparent than a general chatbot response.

However, citation markers do not automatically prove that every sentence is accurate. Students still need to open the cited source and confirm that the AI has interpreted the material correctly.

---

## Hallucination Risk

NotebookLM reduces hallucination risk because it is grounded in uploaded sources, but it does not remove the risk completely. If the uploaded sources are incomplete, outdated or unclear, the output may also be incomplete or misleading.

For academic use, all AI-generated summaries, comparisons and revision questions should be checked against the original sources.

---

## Bias Discussion

Bias may occur if only a limited range of sources is uploaded. For example, investor-focused sources may produce stronger emphasis on financial materiality, while stakeholder-focused sources may produce broader environmental and social analysis.

To reduce bias, students and researchers should upload diverse, current and credible sources.

---

## Limitations Observed

The main limitation observed was that some outputs were too general. NotebookLM gave a useful overview of ESG reporting, but it required a more specific comparison prompt to explain the differences between GRI, IFRS and TCFD.

This shows that NotebookLM output quality depends on:

- quality of uploaded sources
- clarity of the prompt
- source diversity
- user verification

---

## Lecturer and Researcher Perspective

From a lecturer perspective, NotebookLM could help prepare study summaries, weekly reading questions and revision prompts. From a researcher perspective, it could support early theme identification across policy or standards documents.

However, lecturers and researchers should review all AI-generated materials before using them in teaching or research because the tool may simplify complex academic content.

---

## Practical Adoption Considerations

For university adoption, students should be trained to:

- verify citations
- read the original sources
- avoid uploading confidential or restricted documents
- follow academic integrity rules
- acknowledge AI assistance where required
- use NotebookLM as a study support tool rather than a replacement for critical thinking

Universities should also provide clear guidance on privacy, copyright, responsible AI use and acceptable academic use.

---

## Overall Reflection

NotebookLM is feasible for limited use in a university learning ecosystem. It is useful for summarising readings, comparing sources, preparing briefings, creating revision questions and organising themes.

However, it should not be treated as a fully reliable academic authority. Students and lecturers must verify evidence, check citations and use the tool as support for learning rather than as a replacement for academic judgement.
